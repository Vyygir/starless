Did anyone else watch the solar eclipse on the 12th August?

I did. It was one of the rare events that I was excited to witness again.

There isn't much that I really _do_ get excited about these days.

Something technical and new. Code. A potential game. A new series. The usual minutia that give you either a short-term
dopamine rush or fractured disappointment. But those aren't the same as a celestial body displacing true, uniform
light.

Something we depend on so naturally and autonomously, with no expectation of it to ever be gone in our lifetimes.
Completely and utterly disrupted by something else we take for granted. The moon came from the earth and lives a
short, localised life, orbiting us.

Every so often, by no ill-will or intention, it causes an event that's so greatly admired by observers, that natural
human flow is interrupted by it. The norm quickly shifts, just for a moment.

This year didn't start particularly well for me and I don't suspect it to end well either. But, through all of the doom
and gloom, there's still been the tiny fractures of dopamine that come through. A near-total eclipse was one of them.

There's something almost beautifully metaphorical about the entire event.

I couldn't tell you what it is. Probably something about why an insignificant object can, even momentarily, cause such
disruption should enforce that humans can do the same. Or something.

Frankly though, I've been completely demotivated this year.

Don't get me wrong, I've had _ideas_. But ideas are just temporary thoughts or notions or concepts that either dwindle
over time, or end up locked away until it's barely a memory.

My self-confidence for engineering and publicly demonstrating work is at an all time low. I really am my own worst
enemy.

But, amongst it all, I still need to have a purpose or... what's the point? There has to be something to do and, in this
day and age, where we _can_ write terrible software, produce slop, and do the bare minimum, why shouldn't _I_ - someone
who cares about perfectionism more than is healthy - contribute to the growing pool too?

Demotivation comes easier to some of us than anything at all, because we can talk ourselves down. Not off of a ledge.
Just quietly into the notion of what we're attempting to do, having value that doesn't merit the effort. When, in
reality, there is no doing without trying.

By being completely demotivated, again, I've let myself slump lower and lower until my ideas were in the drawer. Again.

But now it's time to open the drawer again and join the hordes of people mass-pushing code onto the internet. Except I
can hope that my perfectionism is an ally instead of a combatant.
